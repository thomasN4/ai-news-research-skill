Jan 21–22

Anthropic publishes Claude's constitution under CC0

CONFIRMED

Anthropic released the full text of the document that shapes Claude's values and behaviour. It is training input rather than a runtime system prompt, and by Anthropic's own framing the final authority on its intentions for the model, with all other guidance meant to be consistent with it. The PDF carries a publication date of January 21; the announcement post is dated January 22. Lead authors are Amanda Askell and Joe Carlsmith, with Chris Olah, Jared Kaplan, Holden Karnofsky, "several Claude models" and other contributors credited. It is released under Creative Commons CC0 1.0 — usable by anyone for any purpose without asking.

On length: Askell has described it as roughly 20,000 words. Secondhand recaps circulating a "90-page" figure are quoting a PDF rendering rather than the authors' own measure; prefer the word count. The document is explicitly scoped to mainline general-access Claude models, and Anthropic notes that some specialised models do not fully fit it — relevant to how the Fable/Mythos-tier safeguards later in this file should be read.

Feb 24–27

Pentagon ultimatum; Anthropic refuses and is designated a supply-chain risk

CONFIRMED

Under a $200M contract signed in July 2025, Claude was the first frontier model cleared for the Pentagon's classified networks (via the Palantir partnership), with the department contractually bound to Anthropic's acceptable use policy. On Feb 24, War Secretary Pete Hegseth met Dario Amodei and demanded the restrictions be dropped so Claude could be used "for all lawful purposes," threatening contract termination, designation as a supply-chain risk, or invocation of the Defense Production Act. Anthropic's stated red lines were mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons. It refused by the 5:01pm Feb 27 deadline. Trump then ordered all federal agencies to stop using Anthropic (six-month wind-down for some), and Hegseth designated the company a supply-chain risk — a label normally reserved for firms tied to foreign adversaries — barring military contractors and suppliers from any commercial dealing with it. Reporting traces the trigger to Anthropic asking whether Claude had been used in the January operation that captured Maduro; Anthropic denies discussing specific operations. Both sides indicated fully autonomous weapons were not actually contemplated under the department's lawful-use framework, making the dispute substantially one about who controls usage policy rather than about a concrete pending use.

Mar (ongoing)

DoW fallout: GSA delisting, contractor spillover, litigation

CONFIRMED

The February designation spread well past the Pentagon. GSA pulled the OneGov agreement and removed Anthropic from USAI.gov and the Multiple Award Schedule, pushing civilian agencies that had piloted Claude (HHS, NASA's JPL, national labs) to unwind. Contractors on DoD work faced certifying non-use. Anthropic sued over the designation, arguing it was legally unsound, that it set a bad precedent for companies negotiating safeguards with the government, and that a DoW supply-chain-risk finding should reach only DoW contract work rather than customers' purely commercial use. Procurement lawyers read the termination route as retaliation and negotiating leverage rather than a genuine national-security measure, and doubted it would survive judicial scrutiny. Anthropic's public line was that punishment would not move it on mass surveillance or autonomous weapons. The episode is the clearest instance in the period of the company accepting a large, immediate revenue loss to hold a stated position — and it foreshadowed June's export-control takedown of Fable 5 and Mythos 5.

May 7

Brussels agrees to delay the high-risk rules, and bans nudification apps

CONFIRMED

Backfilled at v15. This file had no card for the year's biggest EU AI-policy decision. The Council and Parliament reached political agreement on the Digital Omnibus on AI, which the Commission proposed on November 19, 2025. It moves the high-risk rules. Systems used in Annex III areas (biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, migration, asylum and border control) now fall under them from December 2, 2027, and AI built into products such as lifts or toys (Annex I) from August 2, 2028. It adds a new prohibition on AI systems that generate non-consensual intimate material or child sexual abuse material, meaning "nudification" apps. It extends some SME privileges to small mid-caps. And it widens the AI Office's exclusive supervision to AI systems built on a general-purpose model by the same provider, and to AI embedded in very large online platforms and search engines.

Checked against the final text rather than coverage: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, signed at Strasbourg on July 8, published in the Official Journal on July 24 and in force from July 27. Two dates in it are widely misreported. The nudification and CSAM ban does not apply on entry into force; the amended Article 113 applies it from December 2, 2026. And providers of generative systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026 have until December 2, 2026 to meet the Article 50(2) machine-readable marking duty. That second date bears on the August 2 card (see the gaps box).

How to read it: the industry and member-state pressure to delay the Act went somewhere, but narrowly. It moved the high-risk tranche. It did not move the general-purpose AI regime or the original prohibitions, and the AI Office came out with more supervisory reach, not less.

Jun 9

Brussels orders Meta to let rival AI assistants back onto WhatsApp

CONFIRMED

Backfilled at v15. The Commission imposed antitrust interim measures on Meta (Article 102 TFEU, case AT.41034). Meta must restore third-party general-purpose AI assistants' access to the WhatsApp for Business API on the terms in place before October 15, 2025, which means free, within five working days. It must keep that access open until the Commission's final decision. The history: on October 15, 2025 Meta banned third-party general-purpose assistants from the API, leaving Meta AI as the only one on WhatsApp. The Commission opened an investigation in December 2025 and issued a Statement of Objections on interim measures in February 2026. On March 4 Meta let rivals back in, but for a fee the Commission calls "in practice equivalent to the previous access ban." A supplementary Statement of Objections followed in April. This is only the second interim-measures decision under Regulation 1/2003, after Broadcom in 2009. Breaching it risks fines of up to 10% of turnover plus daily penalty payments.

Why it belongs here: this is the first time in this file that the EU bound a frontier lab's conduct on an AI product. It did so through competition law, two months before the AI Act's enforcement powers switched on. The merits are undecided. Press reports that ChatGPT returned to WhatsApp in the EEA in July, but that could not be traced to a primary and is not carried.
